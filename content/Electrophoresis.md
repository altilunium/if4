The motion of charged particles relative to a fluid under the influence of a spatially uniform electric field.

### Etymology
-phoresis (movement of particles), from Ancient Greek "phoréō" (to be carried along)

### Applications
#### Determining Molecular Mass
Electrophoresis is commonly used to estimate the molecular weight of biological macromolecules, such as proteins and nucleic acids. By comparing the movement of unknown samples to standard molecular weight markers, researchers can infer the approximate size of the molecules based on how far they migrate through the gel.

#### Detecting Food Product Forgery or Damage
This technique can help identify adulteration, spoilage, or intentional tampering of food products. For example, it can detect unexpected protein or DNA profiles that indicate substitution of ingredients, contamination, or degradation due to improper storage or processing.

#### Separating Molecules Based on Charge and Size
A fundamental use of electrophoresis is to separate complex mixtures of molecules according to their electrical charge and size. This makes it possible to isolate specific proteins, DNA fragments, or RNA strands for further analysis, purification, or research purposes.

#### Determining the Isoelectric Point (pI)
Electrophoresis can be used to find the isoelectric point of proteins — the pH at which a molecule carries no net electrical charge. This information is vital for protein characterization, purification strategies, and understanding protein behavior in different environments.

#### Identifying and Classifying Biological Molecules
Electrophoresis plays a key role in diagnostics, forensic science, and molecular biology by allowing scientists to identify and classify biomolecules. For instance, it can be used to diagnose genetic disorders, differentiate species, or verify the identity of a biological sample based on its unique electrophoretic pattern.
