"I just arrived in Baku," he said.

Baku is the capital of Azerbaijan and the closest major city to Iran's capital, Tehran. The journey from Tehran to Baku takes about 15 hours by road, typically by bus.

The person who said he had just arrived in Baku is an Indonesian national who had been teaching in Tehran. He was a lecturer in the Southeast Asian Studies postgraduate program at the Faculty of Political Science and Law, University of Tehran.

In line with the Prabowo administration's directive, all Indonesian citizens in Iran were required to leave the country.

On the morning of Friday, June 20, 2025, he left Tehran at 7 a.m. with his wife and their only child. By 7 p.m., they had reached the border with Azerbaijan.

"Why did it take 12 hours? It should’ve only been six."

"We made several stops—for meals and also to pick up fellow Indonesians along the way," he replied.

Their group consisted of around 100 Indonesians, mostly students. They traveled in four buses chartered and paid for by Indonesia’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

The closest land route out of Iran is through its northern neighbor, Azerbaijan. Fortunately, that border remains open.

"We had to wait a very long time at the border—from 7 p.m. until 5 a.m. The passport control queue was extremely long."

At the border, they were met by Indonesia’s ambassador to Iran, Rolliansyah Soemirat. A seasoned diplomat and former Foreign Ministry spokesperson, Soemirat had flown in directly from Jakarta. He had only recently been appointed ambassador by President Prabowo in March 2025, but approval from the Iranian government was still pending. Before that process could be completed, war broke out—making the task of receiving evacuees at the Azerbaijani border his first duty as ambassador.

From the border, it took another five hours by road to reach Baku.

In Baku, the evacuees were put up in a hotel while they waited their turn to be flown back home to Indonesia.
