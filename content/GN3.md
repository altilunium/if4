### History 

Tera Digital Publishing is a Milan based company that produces software for newspapers. [5]

"Basically, we create software that handles all of the production of a newspaper that is not advertising. That is the entire workflow through the system from the budgeting of the paper, to who is going to write what stories, the management of the stories, the control of the resources necessary to write the stories, all the way through to completion. Essentially, it controls a manufacturing process in which you need to coordinate 100's or even 1000's of people to get the product out," said John Juliano, Tera Digital Publishing's reseller relation manager.

There are a number of factors which sets Tera apart within the market. The first unique selling point is that the company produces all of their own core technology. "By way of example, we have our own pagination system. We don't use In-Design. Because we concentrate on the newspaper industry, the product has been tailored for the trade with productivity being the key driver."

Another differentiator lies in the fact that Tera does not customise their software, they configure it to your needs. 

"It is very common for vendors in this business to actually make their profits on customisation, and their sales promotion relies on the fact that they will get you a system that does exactly what you want because they will build it. What many people do not know however is that customisation can triple the cost of the system, and there are normally long lead times to get up and running. At Tera, we can usually get a company fully operational in eight to twelve weeks."

The Tera GN3 product is a media neutral newspaper content management system designed to turn a newsroom into a cohesive unit as opposed to the traditional disjoincted collection of system. 

The next incarnation will be GN4 with a host of new features, and it is expected to be available in the first quarter of 2010.

### Users

* 2001 : Kompas uses the GN3 (Good News 3) production program in its editorial system. The production process is now integrated with the editorial and advertising departments. [1] 
* 2005 : During the first week of July 2005, the South African daily newspaper Sowetan began the migration to the latest version of Tera GN3 that will replace an aging publishing system based on Quark XPress. The system -- 133 work-seats -- comprises a complete GN3 content management system, a Tark digital archiving system, plus a suite of pre-press module for OPI (Open Prepress Interface) and page pairing (arranging pages correctly for printing and binding). [8]
* 2007 : 
	* Media Team Dominicana of the Dominican Republic has purchased a GN3 editorial production and content management system from Tera through its regional reseller Graphic Systems International. The company will use the system to publish its weekly newspaper Clave and the online daily Clave Digital, both of Santo Domingo. Media Team Dominicana intends to establish a new workflow to better leverage the new system, according to Tera. The GN3 software will allow the organization to better manage the news gathering, editing and production processes, with modules for handling wire copy, layout and pagination, work flow, production control and archiving. [2]
	* Central Independent Newspapers has successfully converted editorial production to Tera's GN3 page make-up system. Just four weeks after editorial staff began training, three of the four core titles -- Sutton Coldfield Observer, Lichfield Mercury, and Walsall Advertiser -- were fully converted to GN3 production. Also, almost three-quarters of the flagship Tamworth Herald was also produced using the system. "The only reason we didn't do it all was because we couldn't release everyone for training within the same month," said CIN group editor, Sam Holliday. The close links between the text editing and page make-up application has been identified by Mr Holliday as an ideal chance to introduce reporters into the art of sub-editing. [7]
* 2009 : The Vatican has chosen Tera's GN3 as the new editorial publishing system for its "L'osservatore Romano" newspaper. The paper is now being equipped with 45 seats of GN3's Ted editing software and 17 of its Fred page makeup application, completely replacing a 14-year old system currently being used by a 62-strong editorial team. [6]
	* Tera is on home ground in Italy, producing more than 50 mainly mid-sized titles. "Libero" and "La Padania", two national dailies, are produced using GN3. Milan-based "Libero" uses a 100 seat GN3 system to produce an average 48 daily and over 70 thousand copies a day. "La Padania", the daily Milan newspaper of the Northern League Party, has 50 seats and a nationwide daily circulation of 50,000 copies. 
	* In the south of Italy, two of the most prestigious papers of the area, "La Gazzetta del Sud", based in Messina, and "Gazzetta del Mezzogiorno", based in Bari, use Tera's GN3. Other Italian papers served by Tera's GN3 are "Corriere di Como", "Corriere Adriatico", "Gazzetta di Parma", and "Eco di Biella". In addition, two prestigious free newspapers in Italy are also produced with Tera's GN3 suite of products : "Epiolis" with more than 150 seats and 18 editions daily and "DNews" with more than 30 seats and four regional editions daily.

* 2025 : As of 2025, it has been confirmed that Kompas has already migrated to GN4. [4]
